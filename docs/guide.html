Guide to Blessings

This guide is the not part of the Book of Blessings, but rather a few recommendations on how to administer blessings.

Read the General Introduction

Before you use this app, please take the time to read the General Introduction to the Book of Blessings from the Holy See’s Congregation for Divine Worship. It has some excellent theology about blessings and guidance on how to administer blessings well. In particular, paragraphs 20-27 describe the structure of a blessing, and the words and gestures to be used.

“Say the black, do the red”

When you get to the actual rites of blessing, you will notice that the text includes both red and black text. The black text is what you and those present should say, and the red text is instructions about what to do, what gestures to make, when to pause for prayer, etc.

Read the rite beforehand

Before you give a blessing, please decide which rite you will use, and read the rite through in order to prepare yourself. Read the red instructions carefully, and read the black text out loud to familiarize yourself with the cadence of the words.

If you have time, you can even pray with the text of the rite.

Speak slowly

When you are ready to give the blessing, speak slowly and pause at the appropriate moments. When reading psalms and poetic prayers, pause at the end of each line. This will give your listeners time to process and understand the words. You will probably understand the words much more quickly than your listeners do, both because you have prepared with the rite beforehand and because you are seeing the words on paper. Your listeners will be hearing the words without seeing them, and they may be hearing them for the first time, so they will need extra time to process and understand them.

Liturgical reading requires a slower pace than many other forms of reading and speech. And many people reading in a liturgy feel that they are speaking slowly even though they are actually speaking too quickly.

If you are short on time, there is no need to rush through the words. Most of the blessings offer a shorter form that you may choose. It is better to speak a few words well and meditatively than many words at a restless speed.
