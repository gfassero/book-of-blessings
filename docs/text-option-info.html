Information About the Text Options

This page describes the text options that customize the text from the Book of Blessings.

Presider Mode

The Book of Blessings contains many conditional rubrics that depend on whether the presider is a layperson or a member of the clergy and, in one case, whether the presider is a priest or a deacon. The frequent divergence and convergence of the rubrics may become distracting.

Depending on whether the presider is a layperson, deacon, or priest, the app will show the correct set of rubrics. While there are still some conditional rubrics—e.g., for catechists, for pastors, for parents of children—this setting reduces distractions for the presider.

Lay religious, catechists, acolytes, and lectors should use lay mode. Bishops should use priest mode.

“Simplify rubrics”

The Book of Blessings includes detailed rubrics to guide the presider through the various orders of blessing. In many cases, there are so many rubrics that the page becomes cluttered. Especially for presiders who are familiar with the typical structure of the celebration of a blessing and who have prepared for the blessing in advance—which should be all those presiding at a blessing—the extra rubrics may become distracting.

Enabling this setting will simplify or remove some of the most common and most obvious rubrics. The following is a summary of the rubrics simplified by this text option:

• Simplify rubrics inviting the presider to greet the people: 54 instances
• Remove “The minister says”: 36 instances
• Remove “The celebrant says”: 12 instances
• Remove “The priest says”: 1 instance
• “All make the following or some other suitable reply”: 31 instances
• Becomes “All reply.”
• Remove informational captions on readings: 90 instances
• Simplify rubrics inviting a homily or reflection: 50 instances
• Simplify rubrics introducing intercessions: 50 instances
• Remove “Assisting minister” from between intercessions: 275 instances
• Simplify rubrics introducing prayers of blessing: 2 instances
• Move Trinitarian blessing formula to end of prayers over the people (par. 1277): 1 instance

“Cull options...”

The Book of Blessings offers many options for readings and prayers to be used in blessings. In some cases, there are so many options that the page becomes cluttered.

Enabling this setting will cull out many of the options that seem less useful or less familiar to the faithful. The following is a summary of the options culled by this text option:

• Alternative readings: 224
• Alternative psalms: 63
• Alternative responses to intercessions: 29
• Alternative prayers of blessing: 5
• Alternative responses to greeting: 2

“Use updated translations from Roman Missal...”

The English translation of the Roman Missal has been revised occasionally over recent years in order to reflect the Latin text more faithfully, and most of the faithful have become accustomed to the revised translation. However, the translation of the Book of Blessings has not been revised. As a result, a noticeable difference has evolved between some of the responses in the Book of Blessings and the corresponding responses in the latest translation of the Roman Missal.

Enabling this setting will replace some of the common phrases in the Book of Blessings with the corresponding phrases from the latest translation of the Roman Missal.

Of particular note are the solemn blessings and prayers over the people in the appendix of the Book of Blessings, which are noted as having been taken from the Roman Missal. This option takes the new translations of these blessings and prayers from the Roman Missal.

The following is a summary of the updates made by this text option:

• “And also with you”: 52 instances
Becomes “And with your spirit.”
• “One God, for ever and ever”: 4 instances
• Becomes “God, for ever and ever.”
• “Brothers and sisters, listen to the words of...”: 97 instances
• Becomes “A reading from...”
• Various invitations to prayer during the intercessions: 156 instances
• Become “Let us pray to the Lord.”
• “And may almighty God bless you all”: 33 instances
• Becomes “And may almighty God bless you.”
• Solemn blessings (appendix):
• Six retained (5, 7, 16-19: not found in Roman Missal).
• Nineteen replaced with those in the Roman Missal.
• One added (number 14 from the Roman Missal).
• Prayers over the people (appendix): 26 instances
• Replaced by the 28 in the Roman Missal.
• “May almighty God bless you...” (appendix): 1 instance
• Becomes “And may the blessing of almighty God...come down on you and remain with you for ever.”

“Use new NABRE scripture translations...”

This feature is not yet implemented but will replace the scripture readings in the Book of Blessings with the updated translations of the same scripture passages from the USCCB.

“Add ✠ where missing...”

Some of the blessings in the Book of Blessings do not include the cross symbol, indicating when the presider should make the Sign of the Cross over the other people present. However, the Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments issued a clarification in 2002 (AAS 94, p.684) saying that the celebrant is still expected to make the Sign of the Cross and should do so when the word “bless” or “blessing” appears or, if those words do not appear, then at the end of the prayer of blessing.

Enabling this setting will insert the cross symbol at the appropriate place to remind the celebrant to make the Sign of the Cross over those present. The following is a summary of the insertions made by this text option:

• Crosses already included: 166
• Crosses added: 79
• At mention of blessing: 31
• At end of prayer of blessing: 48
• At “Christ”: 42
• At “for ever”: 5
• Other: 1
